Practical guidance for thoughtful AI use.

Start Here / 5-minute orientation

A clear-eyed start.

AI can help you get from a blank page to a useful first draft. You bring the context, judgment, and final call.

01 / Set expectations

What AI is good at (and bad at)

A useful first pass

AI is often helpful for drafting, reorganizing, brainstorming, summarizing material you provide, and turning a clear request into a starting point.

Not the final authority

It can invent facts, miss context, reproduce bias, or sound certain while being wrong. Check important claims, calculations, sources, and recommendations yourself.

02 / Try something small

Your first three wins

Use low-risk work you already understand. Give the tool a clear goal, then improve the result with your own context.

Draft a message

Turn rough notes into a concise email. Ask for a tone and length, then check every detail.

Get oriented on a topic

Ask for key questions and terms to investigate. Verify the answers using trusted sources.

Break down a plan

Describe a goal and constraints, then ask for a draft sequence of steps and dependencies.

03 / Protect your work

Safe use & data guidelines

  • Use only tools approved for your work, and follow your organization's current policies.
  • Do not enter confidential, personal, regulated, or client data unless the tool and use are explicitly approved.
  • Check outputs before sharing or acting on them, especially facts, numbers, legal guidance, and decisions about people.
  • Keep a person accountable for the result. AI output is a draft, not an approval.

When in doubt, leave sensitive information out and ask your security, privacy, or AI governance contact before proceeding.
